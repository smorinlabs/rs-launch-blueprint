## Findings

No finding requires the decision to change before acceptance. The following
observations are for the next revision or for the controller.

- **O1 — "23 batched pushes" is a model artifact.** The decision reads step
  5b as "23 pushes carried more than one merge" and the script labels the
  figure "batched merges". Re-deriving the ranges from
  `review/evidence/py-paths-verdicts.json` and
  `py-update-contributors-runs.json`: of the 23 multi-commit ranges, 4 contain
  more than one qualifying (non-ignored) commit and 3 are the bot-as-head
  cases; the other 16 are a bot-only commit correctly pushed alone with no run,
  followed by the next qualifying commit, which the partition model folds into
  one range. Those 16 are A2 successes, not batches. Likewise the "94 apparent
  missing runs" of the naive model are mostly pre-guard (86 of 94): before the
  cutover the workflow's history holds only 19 push runs and 46
  `pull_request` runs dated 2026-05-24 to 2026-05-26, so the explanation is
  the post-guard scoping, not push-range semantics. Non-blocking because the
  statements the acceptance rests on ("After the guard: 134 runs for 162
  first-parent commits, zero runs whose push contained only ignored files,
  zero qualifying commits outside a run's range") are accurate and were
  reproduced by both auditors.
- **O2 — py's push-run failure rate is retrieved but not reported.** Of the
  134 post-guard push runs, 122 succeeded and 12 concluded `failure`
  (2026-05-29 to 2026-07-21). The decision reports ts's 3 of 9 schedule
  failures but not py's 12 of 134, although `Tradeoffs accepted` accepts "no
  automatic retry of a failed push run". The figure belongs beside the ts one.
  Non-blocking because the cost is accepted qualitatively and the runner-up's
  winning condition already covers transient failure.
- **O3 — "A5 with `actionlint`" overstates what ran.** A5 has two clauses.
  `actionlint` executed the first (the file is valid). The second (the
  `paths-ignore` list equals the action's `output-file` and `state-file`
  inputs) was not executed by any script; the fixture satisfies it by
  construction, and Opus's proposed check 3 (a `yq` diff) exists but was not
  run. Non-blocking; a one-line wording fix or a two-line addition to
  `run-check.sh`.
- **O4 — Disagreement 3 is not a disagreement.** Codex's "moot" means a path
  filter has no role once the trigger is time-based, and Codex's recommended
  block omits `paths-ignore` entirely; it never proposed a filter under
  `schedule`. Step 3 shows the key is invalid there, which both raws agree
  with. Non-blocking; the MEDIUM answer is right.
- **O5 — Empirical-auditor fixture location.** The empirical-auditor
  contract asks for "a fresh fixture under `review/empirical-audit/`";
  audit-codex reran from `/private/tmp/r07-empirical-audit.EFglwU/empirical`
  and no `review/empirical-audit/` directory exists. The rerun itself is
  faithful (its log matches the synthesizer's from step 1 onward apart from
  paths). For the controller at packaging, since `identities.json` records the
  rerun cwd.
- **O6 — Visibility does not "cut the same way" for ts.** See `## Principle
  and agreement level`. ts is private, so the auto-disable rule that decides
  rs does not currently apply to it; the sentence should say so, and the
  cross-repo rationale for ts should lean on the README, the header
  concession and the observed delays and failures. Non-blocking for rs.
- **O7 — A3 and A4 are more supported than "proposed" suggests.** py's real
  history for the identical block holds 3 `workflow_dispatch` runs, and the
  three bot-as-head runs were created 9 s to 86 s after their head commit.
  The decision leaves A3 and A4 wholly proposed, which is conservative rather
  than wrong.
