## Findings

No unresolved findings. Four observations, none blocking, all for P03 or a later revision rather than for this one.

**O1 — the offline/network-failure path is asserted as a class and executed only for the manifest-error member; it is absent from the edge cases handed to P03. Does not block.** `## Decision` says "Cargo's own failures (manifest errors, cache or network errors) keep Cargo's diagnostic and Cargo's exit status (101), distinct from a stale lock", and essential behavior 6 carries the same class claim. Only the manifest-error instance is executed (F4). Nothing false is asserted — Cargo exits 101 on error generally, F4 demonstrates the class, and `raw/codex.md:133` explicitly weighed and rejected an automatic `--offline` fallback because "a cold offline failure means validation could not finish, not necessarily that the lockfile is stale". The gap is placement, not truth: the operational consequence — a developer working offline with a cold registry index cannot commit a manifest edit even when the committed lock is fresh — appears in neither the "Edge cases any implementation must handle" list nor the unproven list, although both A5 and A5b run with a cold cache *and* network. P03 should either name it as a disclosed limit or decide the `--offline` policy deliberately; a later revision could add one line to each list without touching a design choice.

**O2 — the transient lock write is disclosed, but only its concurrency consequence. Does not block.** The limits paragraph says the check "transiently writes a tracked `Cargo.lock` inside the hook before restoring it … so a manager running jobs in parallel must not run another Cargo-writing job concurrently". The other consequence of the same property — an interrupted hook (SIGINT during the resolve, a killed terminal) leaves the tracked lockfile modified in the worktree, which essential behavior 2 promises never happens — is not named. It follows from what is disclosed and is P03's trap-handling concern, not a claim defect.

**O3 — a quoted R23 parenthetical is truncated without an ellipsis. Does not block.** The decision quotes R23 as "(how and where that guard is wired — hook, CI step or job — is owned by R41)"; R23's text continues "`lockfile-freshness-check`, still open)". The substance is unchanged and R41 is indeed still open. Worth an ellipsis if the text is ever edited again; not worth a revision on its own.

**O4 — for the controller, carried forward from the r1 judgment audit's F5. Does not block.** The decision routes CI placement of `--all` and the `--locked` invocations to R11's `ci-job-structure`, which R41 neither owns nor consumes; the chain works only because R37 (F174) consumes that parameter. When R11 and R37 run, their inputs should see R41's supplied command, trigger set and `--all` mode.

Judged as research rather than as an implementation, this decision does what P02 is for: it establishes three properties of Cargo and Git that no raw report and no evidence check found, each surfaced by an audit and then reproduced on four toolchain/OS legs; it derives every element of its recommendation from one of them; it compares fourteen alternatives including both designs it abandoned, each with an executed discriminator; and it states a guarantee whose scope clauses correspond one-to-one to the mechanism's real limits, with the not-covered set named in the same sentence. The claim about R23 is accurate to R23's own text, the ledger ownership is accurate to `COMMONALITY.md`, and the cited archived-audit figures (the 647-of-890 vendored-crate count, the `uv lock --check` probe) are accurate to those audits. I found nothing asserted beyond its evidence.

Declining to rerun and demoting the script to an illustrative sketch are correct under the owner's 2026-09-10 scope ruling, and the unproven list — ten items, each with what would prove it — is the deliverable that ruling asks for, not a deficiency. Eight revisions is not, by itself, evidence of a weak decision here: five of them changed or hardened the design under audit pressure and three were spent on statements that described the document as narrower or wider than its own diffs. That third class is now closed, and I re-derived the substantive content independently rather than relying on the r8 hash comparison.

**One defect-shaped item is a design question for the owner, not a fixable gap, and I name it as such.** The rejection of alternative (J) — limit the guarantee to the top-level workspace — is argued entirely on runtime process cost ("discovery costs a single-workspace repository nothing", A4/A4b) and on the dishonesty of the alternative's two honest forms (a silent gap or a hard block on nested locks). It does not weigh the other cost §2 lists among the comparison criteria: the implementation and maintenance burden P03 inherits. Root discovery, `cargo locate-project --workspace` validation per candidate, per-root `cd`, Git-ignored-lock detection and below-root-config reporting are novel Bash with no maintained external implementation to adopt, carried for a template whose own shape has exactly one workspace and may never have another. The research supports either answer: the nested-workspace false pass is real and reproduced (G1a/G1b), and so is the fact that the template does not present that shape. The question — *does the template's hook support nested independent workspaces, or refuse a `Cargo.lock` outside the top-level workspace and say so?* — is the owner's to settle when reviewing this proposal, and the decision has given them what they need to settle it. It is not a defect in the research and does not affect this verdict.
