## Findings

Unresolved finding (blocking; the decision must change before acceptance):

1. **The `re-verify:` trigger had already fired when the decision was written, and gate 3 did not audit the dependency tree it names.** The decision's `re-verify:` line (a required field) names "a RustSec advisory for `committed` or `git2`" as a trigger, and gate 3 names `git2` as the one FFI dependency. Evidence gathered on 2026-09-05: the published `committed-1.1.11.crate` (from `https://static.crates.io/crates/committed/committed-1.1.11.crate`) and the `v1.1.11` tag both lock `git2 0.20.4` and `libgit2-sys 0.18.3+1.9.2`, so `cargo install --locked`, the release assets and the action all carry that version. `https://rustsec.org/packages/git2.html` lists RUSTSEC-2026-0183 (`Remote::list()`, 2026-05-12) and RUSTSEC-2026-0184 (buffer-created `BlameHunk` signatures, 2026-05-13), both `informational = "unsound"` and `patched = [">= 0.21.0"]`; RUSTSEC-2026-0008 (patched `>= 0.20.4`) is satisfied. `cargo audit --file committed-1.1.11/Cargo.lock` (cargo-audit 0.22.1, 181 dependencies) reports zero vulnerabilities and prints `warning: 3 allowed warnings found`: the two git2 entries plus RUSTSEC-2026-0190 (`anyhow 1.0.100`, `Error::downcast_mut()`, patched `>= 1.0.103`); the same command with `--deny warnings` exits 1. The upstream fix is blocked on `committed` itself, because its manifest pins `git2 = "0.20"` and 0.21 is a semver-incompatible bump. Bound of the finding: `committed`'s own source calls only `git2::Repository::discover`, `git2::Revwalk`, `git2::Commit` and `git2::Oid`, contains no `remote`, `blame`, `Buf` or `downcast` call, and has zero `unsafe`, so none of the three advisories is reachable through this tool; the pick, the owned parameter value and every empirical result stand. Required change, text only: (a) gate 3 records the `cargo audit` result and the reachability argument; (b) the `re-verify:` trigger becomes "a vulnerability-class RustSec advisory for `committed` or its locked tree, or an unsound advisory in an API `committed` calls"; (c) the upgrade-gate paragraph notes that the next `committed` release is expected to move to `git2 >= 0.21` and must pass the recorded gate. Under RUNBOOK §6 the controller reruns an item when its trigger fires, so publishing the trigger as written would invalidate the decision on its first quarterly check.

## Assessment

The decision is a well-argued, evidence-led substitution: it states the shared principle at the right level, compares real architectural alternatives before libraries, selects on capability and gates rather than on repository agreement or popularity, proves the contract with an executed fixture that a second model family reproduced, and records its gaps (no separate footer knob, no subject-case rule, linear branches, the `hard_line_length` defect) as deliberate properties. Every figure I re-fetched matched.

It cannot be accepted as written because its own re-verification control is mis-armed: the trigger it publishes is already satisfied by three informational advisories in the pinned dependency tree that the decision did not detect, and gate 3 does not show the dependency-tree audit for the FFI dependency it names. The correction is confined to gate 3, the `re-verify:` line and the upgrade-gate note; nothing in the pick, the parameter or the empirical record changes, and the existing fixture need not be re-designed.

On the absent Doxa report: it leaves no conclusion unsupported. The only single-source figures are the ten-issue responsiveness medians and the adopter enumeration for candidates that are not the pick, both immaterial to the outcome; the tool choice rests on source re-verification and executed behavior, and the crates.io, tag, manifest and advisory facts were re-checked here. Whether the three-engine pilot may be accepted on two engines is a controller and owner policy question under RUNBOOK §2 and §6, outside this audit's verdict.
