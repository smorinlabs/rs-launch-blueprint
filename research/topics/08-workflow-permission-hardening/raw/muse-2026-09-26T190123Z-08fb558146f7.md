### Principles and implementation

**Shared requirement, source, and agreement level.** The principle both source repos serve — one broadly (ts), one narrowly (py) — is: *a workflow must run with the least privilege its jobs need, and must not leave ambient credentials where a later compromised step can reach them*. Source: F028 (`COMMON → REUSE`, both repos) for the permission half; F029/F030 (`DIVERGENT`) for the two open questions. Agreement is required at the level of **policy plus observable acceptance criteria**, not byte-identical YAML: (1) every workflow file declares deny-all at the top and re-grants the minimum per job; (2) every `actions/checkout` step sets `persist-credentials: false` unless its job's documented purpose is to push; (3) a CI gate fails any workflow that violates (1) or (2). What must agree: the three rules and their exceptions policy. What may vary: comment wording, allowlist mechanics, and which enforcement tool runs first.

**HIGH (owner clarification): principles across py, ts, and rs.** The engineering principle (least privilege + credential non-persistence) is platform-level — it belongs to GitHub Actions, not to any language ecosystem — so **the same tradeoff is justified in all three ecosystems and no ecosystem difference applies**. py's narrow `persist-credentials` (one shared template workflow) and its explicit top-level contributors grant are accidental drift, not justified differences: both repos run the same platform with the same threat model. ts's broad application is the evidence-backed shape (the zizmor `artipacked` mitigation). Recommendation for the follow-on py/ts alignment project: adopt the same three rules; the rationale is the artipacked attack class plus mechanical enforceability, both platform facts. No `BASELINE-REVIEW:` arises: F028 is respected as inherited, and F029/F030 are this item's own open questions, not challenges to another item's baseline.

**HIGH Q1 — broad or narrow `persist-credentials: false`?** Broad: every checkout step sets it by default. Three grounds. First, the threat is ambient, not per-workflow: with the default `true`, the checkout step writes a credential into `.git/config`, and *any* later step in the job — including a compromised third-party action — inherits it. A "demonstrated risk" standard inverts the burden: compromise is demonstrated after the fact. Second, zizmor's `artipacked` audit (prior knowledge, re-verify) flags exactly the persisting checkout, so broad application is what the reference auditor checks; narrow application would need per-workflow suppressions, each a new review burden. Third, the cost of broad application is one line per checkout step plus a documented exception for push jobs (MEDIUM answer) — no behavior change for read-only jobs. Current guidance (GitHub hardening docs; zizmor audit catalogue — both prior knowledge, re-verify) recommends minimizing credential exposure; synthesis must confirm the citations.

**HIGH Q2 — contributors-bot: deny-all or explicit top-level grant?** Deny-all-plus-re-grant, no exception. py's explicit top-level grant (`contents/pull-requests/issues: write`, no deny-all wrapper) is *functionally* equivalent today: the three grants are the whole permission set either way. It is rejected on uniformity and mechanical-check grounds, not on privilege grounds. The deny-all top level is a file-level invariant a reviewer — or a five-line YAML check, executed in this run's probe — can verify without understanding the job: "top level is `{}`, every job declares its own". One exempt workflow breaks that invariant and forces every future audit to special-case it. The contributors job's three write grants are re-granted at the job level, exactly as ts does. Cost: three moved lines, zero privilege change.

**MEDIUM — where `persist-credentials: false` breaks a legitimate step.** Yes: any job whose purpose is to push (release publish, docs deploy, and the contributors bot itself *if* it pushes with `GITHUB_TOKEN`). The rule therefore carries a documented exception, narrow by construction: a checkout may omit `persist-credentials: false` only when (a) its job's `permissions` include `contents: write`, (b) the job's documented purpose requires pushing through the checked-out remote, and (c) an inline comment names the reason. The probe implements this as a job-name allowlist (`push-docs` passes, `check` without the flag fails). Preferred alternative where the platform allows: keep `persist-credentials: false` and authenticate the push explicitly (e.g. `gh auth login` with a short-lived token), so the exception set can shrink to zero over time.

**LOW — mechanical enforcement.** Yes, two layers. Primary: zizmor in CI (`artipacked` for F029, `excessive-permissions` for F030/F028 conformance — audit names prior knowledge, re-verify), which R11 already slots into the always-run `hygiene` job, so no new job topology is needed. Secondary: a repo-local YAML policy check (top-level `== {}`, every job has `permissions`, every checkout sets the flag modulo the push exception) — executed in this run's probe: PASS on both good fixtures, three findings on the bad fixture. `actionlint` was executed on all three fixtures and passed all three, confirming it covers syntax only and cannot substitute for either layer.

**Architectural alternatives compared.** (i) Default-true with per-step opt-out (status quo ante): rejected — insecure default, unauditable. (ii) Repository-level `GITHUB_TOKEN` restriction to read-only via org/repo settings: complementary, not a substitute — it sets a ceiling but cannot express per-job shape or checkout flags, and template consumers may not control org settings. (iii) OIDC-to-cloud with no `GITHUB_TOKEN` at all: out of proportion — removes the token for cloud deploys but not for contents/pull-requests/issues operations the template needs. The recommended stack is (policy-as-YAML + zizmor + local check); (ii) is recommended as a documented consumer setting, not a template file.

**Minimal realistic example and acceptance check.** Three fixtures (good `ci`, good contributors-bot, bad `ci`) plus the probe script; see Validation strategy. Executed results are recorded there; the zizmor-on-fixtures run and the template-workflow application are planned, not executed.

### Recommendation

One stack: (1) `persist-credentials: false` on every checkout step by default, with a documented push-job exception; (2) deny-all-plus-re-grant `permissions` on every workflow including the contributors bot, with no exceptions; (3) zizmor (`artipacked`, `excessive-permissions`) in the always-run `hygiene` job plus a repo-local YAML policy check as the mechanical gate.

#### persist-credentials-false by default (checkout credential hardening)

##### Landscape
Member category: checkout-step credential hygiene. First-party control: the `persist-credentials` input on `actions/checkout` (default `true`, persisting a token into `.git/config`). Industry-standard detector: zizmor's `artipacked` audit (prior knowledge, re-verify). No up-and-comer found. Authority: the ts evidence note naming artipacked; zizmor adoption figures (top-level Landscape, source 3). Practice: ts broad (`ci.yml:57`, `codeql.yml:101-102`, `publish.yml:51-52`, `dependency-review.yml:57-58`, `manual-pr-security-scan.yml:67-68` per prompt); py narrow (`difftree-pr-comment.yml:55` only).

##### Principles and implementation
Implements the credential-non-persistence half of the shared principle: no job step may inherit a checkout credential it did not request. Mechanism: one `with: persist-credentials: false` line per checkout step; the push-job exception (MEDIUM rule) is the only carve-out, requiring `contents: write` + documented push purpose + inline comment. This preserves F028's least-privilege intent at the credential layer and matches what zizmor checks, so policy and auditor agree by construction.

##### Dominant choice
Broad default-on (ts shape): every checkout step in every workflow sets `persist-credentials: false` unless it qualifies for the push exception.

##### Qualified shortlist
Broad default-on (recommended — matches the reference auditor, one-line cost, ambient-threat rationale). Narrow py-style (qualified: sufficient where no third-party action ever follows checkout, but fragile — any added step silently re-expands the blast radius — and requires per-workflow suppressions under zizmor).

##### Excluded by gate
Crate fitness gates 1–6: `inapplicable` — this member adds no crate, no dependency tree, no compiled code, so there is no license to check, no MSRV, no advisory surface, no OS build matrix, no features/runtime, and no binary-size/compile-time cost. Excluded on the item's own acceptance criterion instead: default-`true` everywhere (no hardening) fails criterion (2) and leaves the artipacked vector open in every job.

##### Up-and-comers

##### Fit for this template
Exact fit. The policy is product-shape-independent (CLI · library · web service all check out code the same way) and OS-independent (YAML). It applies uniformly to whatever job topology R11's accepted `ci-job-structure` defines, since it constrains steps, not jobs.

##### Recommendation
Adopt broad default-on with the MEDIUM push-job exception, documented in a comment convention the local check enforces.

##### Ranked runner-up

Broad costs one line per checkout plus exception bookkeeping; it risks breaking a push job whose author forgot the exception — mitigated because the failure is loud (push denied) and local (that job only), and the local check names the exact step. Narrow saves those lines but keeps a silent, job-wide credential wherever it is not applied — a quiet failure mode, the worse direction.
